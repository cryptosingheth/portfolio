vanna.finance

The credit layer DeFi was missing: I named it, shaped every part of it, and funded it.

Anyone running a real strategy on-chain had to fund every venue separately, post collateral at each and watch a separate liquidation line at each. Prime brokers solved that off-chain decades ago by holding a client’s whole book in one account and lending against net exposure; DeFi had hundreds of venues and nothing above them. With my cofounder I set out to build the missing layer: one margin account that sees every position, extends undercollateralised credit against net exposure and keeps the book solvent. There was no product to copy, so I worked out who would reach credit and how (traders in an app, businesses over an API, agents over MCP) and then shaped every surface, from the design system up. The risk model, the tokenomics and the revenue-first business model were built with my cofounder, and twice, at the time, we walked away from a venue rather than extend credit against positions we could not mark.

Hardening toward a guarded mainnet; perps and options next.

github.com/cryptosingheth/shieldlend

I wrote the circuits, the browser proofs and the on-chain programs myself.

After the ZK certification I wanted to prove the thesis in code: a borrower should be able to show they are collateralised without revealing what they hold. ShieldLend does that twice. On EVM, Circom circuits produce Groth16 proofs in the browser, with Pedersen commitments, Merkle membership and nullifiers, and a range proof that the collateral ratio clears the threshold while the amounts stay hidden; verification runs through zkVerify at roughly a tenth of the L1 cost. On Solana, three Anchor programs verify Groth16 on-chain, with privacy layered across the whole transaction lifecycle.

EVM: the circuits and the proofs.
  • Circom circuits; snarkjs proofs generated in the browser
  • Pedersen commitments, Merkle membership, nullifiers
  • Collateral range proof: ratio above threshold, amounts hidden
  • zkVerify verification, ~91% cheaper than on-chain L1
Solana: three Anchor programs.
  • On-chain Groth16 verification over BN254, on devnet
  • MagicBlock private execution and VRF
  • IKA 2PC-MPC dWallet relay; Encrypt FHE state
  • Umbra stealth exits; Colosseum Frontier 2026
What I decided.
  • Privacy is a stack, not a feature: layered across the lifecycle
  • Prove the ratio, never the balance
  • Verify off-L1 where it is cheaper and still trustless

Where this is going

Money, agents and proofs are converging. This is where I think each is going, and why I build at the point where they meet.

  1. 01

    Digital assets

    Tokenised stocks, treasuries, gold and compute will be ordinary balance-sheet items by the end of the decade, held in accounts that look like banking and settle on-chain.

    The winners will not be the chains. They will be the products that make a regulated money surface feel simple without hiding the risk.

  2. 02

    DeFi

    On-chain credit is still either overcollateralised or trapped in one venue. The next cycle belongs to the layer above the venues: portfolio margin, credit extended against net exposure, risk engines that see every position.

    That is the piece traditional finance built decades ago and DeFi still lacks. It is what Vanna is for.

  3. 03

    Agents

    Agents will hold money, not metaphorically: margin accounts, bank accounts, gold. Within five years most of the capital that moves on-chain will be moved by software acting for a person or a business.

    The product is not the model. It is the guardrails: spending policies checked before money moves, a health factor an agent cannot talk past, a person who signs.

  4. 04

    Privacy

    Enterprises and institutions will not put their books on public rails without selective disclosure. Zero-knowledge proofs move from research to product: prove solvency, eligibility or compliance without revealing the position.

    Privacy becomes the bedrock requirement, and the product leaders who know what a proof costs will build those products.

Where they converge

Put the four together and you get one picture: agents holding tokenised money, borrowing against it through composable credit, inside limits their owners set, with proofs in place of disclosures. Every product I have shaped since 2023 sits on that line: Vanna’s margin account for humans and agents, Auri’s gold account with agent rails, Perflo’s spending policies, the approval gates inside a manufacturing group. That is where I am going, and where I want to build next.
