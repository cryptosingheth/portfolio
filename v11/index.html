vanna.finance

The credit layer DeFi was missing: I named it, shaped every part of it, and funded it.

Anyone running a real strategy on-chain had to fund every venue separately, post collateral at each and watch a separate liquidation line at each. Prime brokers solved that off-chain decades ago by holding a client’s whole book in one account and lending against net exposure; DeFi had hundreds of venues and nothing above them. With my cofounder I set out to build the missing layer: one margin account that sees every position, extends undercollateralised credit against net exposure and keeps the book solvent. There was no product to copy, so I worked out who would reach credit and how (traders in an app, businesses over an API, agents over MCP) and then shaped every surface, from the design system up. The risk model, the tokenomics and the revenue-first business model were built with my cofounder, and twice, at the time, we walked away from a venue rather than extend credit against positions we could not mark.

Hardening toward a guarded mainnet; perps and options next.

github.com/cryptosingheth/shieldlend

I wrote the circuits, the browser proofs and the on-chain programs myself.

After the ZK certification I wanted to prove the thesis in code: a borrower should be able to show they are collateralised without revealing what they hold. ShieldLend does that twice. On EVM, Circom circuits produce Groth16 proofs in the browser, with Pedersen commitments, Merkle membership and nullifiers, and a range proof that the collateral ratio clears the threshold while the amounts stay hidden; verification runs through zkVerify at roughly a tenth of the L1 cost. On Solana, three Anchor programs verify Groth16 on-chain, with privacy layered across the whole transaction lifecycle.

Outcomes of my work

  • A working privacy-native lending prototype on two chains
  • Proofs generated in the browser, no trusted server
  • Verification cost cut ~91% by moving it to zkVerify
  • Privacy across the whole transaction lifecycle on Solana

My thesis

By 2030 regulated money settles on-chain, agents spend it under policy, and proofs carry the disclosures. I build where the three meet.

  1. 1Digital assets: regulated money moves on-chain

    Tokenised deposits settle inside the largest banks, tokenised treasuries are past $15 billion, and collateral moves between custodian and exchange around the clock. Banks and asset managers go first on permissioned rails with qualified custody; fintechs arrive through stablecoins under GENIUS, MiCA and Canada’s Stablecoin Act.

  2. 2DeFi: credit against the whole book

    Perp DEXs clear a tenth of crypto perpetual volume, yet on-chain credit is still overcollateralised and siloed by venue. The layer above the venues wins: one margin account, portfolio margin on net exposure, a live risk engine. Funds adopt it first, fintechs embed it over an API, banks follow once collateral is tokenised deposits.

  3. 3AI agents: software gets a spending limit

    Visa, Mastercard and Stripe sit inside the x402 Foundation; agent wallets, MCP tool access and spending policies are live, and ERC-8004 gives an agent an on-chain identity and reputation. The product is the policy layer: know-your-agent, per-task limits, allowlists, and a named person signing above a threshold.

  4. 4Privacy: prove it, never publish it

    Institutions will not put their books on public rails without selective disclosure, and the rules are moving that way: the EU identity wallet ships by end-2026 and proof of reserves is becoming table stakes. Viewing keys, privacy pools and range proofs make compliant privacy the default; compliance officers, not cryptographers, decide what is shown.

Where they converge

  • Agents spend under policy

    An agent with an allowance, an allowlist and a session budget, paying over x402 or a card rail for exactly what its owner permitted.

    I framed this: Perflo
  • Credit against net exposure

    A trader, a business or an agent borrows against its whole book in one margin account, under a health factor no prompt can override.

    I build here: Vanna
  • Prove it, never publish it

    A desk proves it is collateralised, eligible or screened with a zero-knowledge proof and a viewing key for the regulator; the book stays private.

    I prototyped this: ShieldLend
  • Agents inside the ERP

    Agents draft the order, the invoice match and the posting; a named approver signs above a limit; every write is logged once, in the client’s cloud.

    I build here: the Nahar programme
